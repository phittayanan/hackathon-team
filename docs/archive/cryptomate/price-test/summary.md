# Price test: summary

This belongs to the dropped CryptoMate idea (see [decisions](../../../decisions.md)); kept for reference.

50 items, cheapest delivered to Bangkok: Thai shops versus shops abroad.
Items: [items.md](items.md). Every row: [results.md](results.md).
Prices read 2026-10-15. 1 USD = 33.62 THB.

## Read this first

- This session could not open any shop page. Every price is from a search
  result, tagged [search]. Numbers can be old or promo prices.
- No search result showed an international shipping fee for an item. So the
  world price is a **floor**: item + duty + 7% VAT, with shipping counted as 0
  unless a shop's published fee was found (two rows).
- The result is still useful. If Thailand wins against the floor, it wins
  for real, because shipping only makes the world price higher.

### Cheaper abroad after all costs

- **Proven: 1 of 35 compared items.** Victorinox Huntsman (46): Amazon.de
  1,740 THB delivered with the Amazon.de fee, versus 2,290 THB in Thailand.
  World saves 550 THB (24%). But Amazon may refuse to ship a blade to
  Thailand, so even this one is not sure.

- The published fees we did find are larger than most break-even numbers:
  Amazon.de to Asia starts at EUR 18 + EUR 8/kg (about 710 THB for a small
  parcel), Waterstones GBP 13 (about 580 THB). With fees like these, only
  the Kindle and the Pokémon bundle stay clearly cheaper abroad. The Keychron
  is on the edge. Atomic Habits could
  win if Wordery's free worldwide delivery covers Thailand (not confirmed).
  LEGO.com US probably does not ship to Thailand.
- **Categories where abroad can win**: items with no official Thai store
  (Kindle), collectibles and hobby gear (Pokémon cards, LEGO, a brand-store
  keyboard), a gaming controller. All are bought rarely. The typical world
  saving there is 15-24% before shipping.

### Cheaper in Thailand

- **25 of 35 compared items** are cheaper in Thailand at the stated duty. 18
  of them stay cheaper even with free shipping and 0% duty.
- That includes almost all items bought often: chargers, earbuds, memory
  cards, a mouse, Korean and Japanese skincare, coffee. It also includes
  expensive items. Even against the 0% duty floor, Thailand is cheaper by at
  least 4,806 THB for the Sigma lens, 2,121 THB for the Seiko watch and
  1,519 THB for the DJI Osmo Pocket 3.
- Duty decides 7 items (2, 15, 24, 25, 28, 31, 42). The duty rates there are
  from third-party sites. For the Sony WH-1000XM5 the source says 60%, which
  looks like a ceiling rate. At 0% duty the world floor would be 3,367 THB
  cheaper, so this item needs the real Thai Customs rate.

## Saving between Thai shops

This is the saving the agent's own compare finds: the cheapest Thai shop
versus the dearest Thai shop for the same product. It comes from the Thai
prices named in each row's notes. We did not search again for it.

- **33 items** had two or more Thai prices for the same product. In 28 of
  them the prices differed.
- **Typical saving**: median 225 THB (20%) across those 28 items. Across all
  33, the median is 140 THB (18%).
- **Biggest savings**:

- The biggest saving in percent is a small item: the Anker 511 charger (1),
  350 THB at JIB versus 790 THB at Mercular (126%).
- So the Thai-shop compare saves more, and more often, than buying abroad.
  It works for items bought often (15 of the 28) and rarely (13 of 28).
- The dearest price can be a list price that no one pays. Treat these
  savings as an upper case.
- Two rows may have an even lower Thai price than the one we used. Item 34
  has 2,290 THB on an old page. Item 31 has a 2,555 THB sale with no date.
  We kept the current prices.

## Delivery time

- Thai shops: only one row showed a delivery time (LEGO, 2-5 working days).
  Thai delivery times were not checked.
- Shops abroad, from published shipping pages: Amazon.com standard to Asia
  9-20 days, Amazon.de 7-19 working days (Expedited 7-14), Waterstones 7-10
  working days from dispatch, Kibi Sports (India) 10-20 business days.
- So the world option is about one to three weeks. It also adds customs
  steps.

## What this means for the pitch

1. For everyday buys, Thai shops are cheaper once tax is in. The agent should
   not promise "cheaper from abroad".
2. The honest saving is the agent comparing many shops on the real total,
   shipping included.
3. Abroad helps only for rare items with no good Thai seller, and the agent
   must show shipping, duty and VAT before the owner approves.

## What could not be checked

- **No page was opened.** Every price is [search]. Some snippets were old,
  promo or sold out. The notes in each row say so.
- **International shipping fees** per item. Only two rows have a published
  fee (Waterstones, Amazon.de). AmazonGlobal fees to Thailand, iHerb,
  AliExpress, StyleKorean, B&H, Adorama, Keychron and British Corner Shop
  fees were not found.
- **Thai domestic shipping** was not shown for most listings. It counts as 0.
- **Duty rates** are from third-party sites, not the Thai Customs tariff.
  Some look like ceiling rates. We fixed four clear cases to 0% (books, HS
  8471). The "floor at 0% duty" column in `results.md` shows the other end.
- **Courier clearance fees and card FX fees** are not included. They make
  the world price higher.
- **15 items could not be compared**: mostly food, household and some
  skincare had no shop abroad shown to ship to Thailand. That itself is a
  result: for these, buying abroad is hard.
- **Shipping limits** (batteries, liquids, blades) were noted but not tested
  at checkout.
- **Item 40** (Yonex racket) has an Indian price in INR. We had no INR rate
  from the same ECB table, so it was not converted.
- Model updates: some exact models are replaced in Thai stores (Apple 20W
  MHJA3, EarPods MTJY3, Anker 313 model number). The notes say which.
